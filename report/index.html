Rube's take

October's story isn't new construction. It's South Florida fixing what it already has.

Renovation and addition permits rose 18% from September, roofing 14%, and windows and doors 14%. New homes were just 4% of all permits. The busiest zip codes say the same thing: South Miami Heights (+27%), Allapattah (+33%) and West Little River (+24%) are established neighborhoods, and together they pulled 762 permits, only 26 of them for new construction.

The typical job is modest, too. The median building permit was $19,000, roughly where it was a year ago.

My read: owners are staying put and putting money into roofs, windows, and additions instead of moving. The trend may show growth in multigenerational living (moving grandparents into an ADU instead of a nursing facility). High insurance costs also reward hardening a home. Permits can't always give a definitive answer, but they help to paint a picture.

For suppliers, that means steady small and mid-size orders from remodelers. For lenders and insurers, it's a block-by-block map of where homes are being upgraded.

— Rube Hollis

How this is made: permits issued in October 2026, from public permit records.

  • Miami-Dade County: every permit in the county's public building-permits-issued dataset.
  • City of Miami: every permit in the city's public building-permit dataset, whatever its current status. Sub-permits for one project are counted once.

Declared value is the construction cost listed on building permits, not a sale price. Electrical, plumbing and mechanical permits, and sub-permits filed under a master permit, often repeat the whole project value, so they are counted as permits but left out of value totals and big projects. Trade permits are also left out of the contractor ranking.
