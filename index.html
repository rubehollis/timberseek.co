What Timberseek does

Three jobs the data does for your sales team.

No tags, no fluff. The product is a prioritized list of builders starting projects in your territory, ranked by how likely they are to be buying framing material now.

01 — Intelligence

Live permit coverage.

We pull from 48 county and city permit portals across 20 states. New homes, additions, and remodels. Each run reports only the permits you haven't seen before.

02 — Prioritization

Scored on what the permit says.

Four signals, all from the permit record: how far along the job is, how much lumber the work uses (new construction, remodel or outdoor), how many jobs the builder has going, and whether contact details are on file. Scores run from 0 to 50.

03 — Timing

Follow the job after the permit.

Every source's permit status is mapped to the same stages (submitted, issued, inspection, final), so a builder's jobs read the same in any market, and newly issued jobs rank first. In Florida, Timberseek also matches Notice of Commencement filings.

How it works

From public record to warm lead in three steps.

1.

Pull

We query county and city permit portals on ArcGIS, Socrata, CKAN, and OpenDataSoft, and normalize every record to one format: contractor, job address, permit type, and status.

2.

Score

Each permit is scored from 0 to 50 on its stage, lumber tier, builder activity and contact details. A project's sub-permits count once, and trade-only permits are set aside. Contact enrichment is available as an option: email and phone from the permit where listed, or a Google business lookup when only a name is on file.

3.

Deliver

A prioritized lead list in CSV, grouped by how you can reach each contractor (email and phone, phone only, or name only), plus a market report that ranks your markets and the builders in them.

What you get

More than a list of permits.

The same permit data, worked into the three things a sales team asks for first: where to sell, who to call, and leads that are worth the call.

Market reports

See where the lumber is going.

Every market and every builder in it is ranked by lumber-weighted permit activity, so a county full of new homes outranks one full of kitchen remodels. Job values come with each market where the city publishes them.

Your search

Built around what you sell.

When you start, we capture your job profile: the job types you supply, the permit stages you want to hear about, job size, and the markets you cover. Your lead list is tuned to it.

Clean leads

Worth the phone call.

No electricians, pool contractors or permit-filing services. A project with ten sub-permits counts once, remodels are not counted as new homes, and mistyped job values are dropped.
