What Timberseek does

A plain list goes stale by week six. The feed leads with what changed.

You already know most of the builders in your area. The Builder Feed opens every week with the ones you don't, and tells you what your own accounts are up to.

01 — What changed

New builders, first.

Each issue leads with builders new to your delivery radius or new to the market, builders whose permit count jumped, and new subdivisions: three or more permits by one builder on the same street in a month.

02 — Account Watch

Know when an account goes quiet.

Send us your current builder accounts at signup. Each week the feed shows who is pulling more permits, who has gone quiet (often a sign they moved to another supplier), and who is building outside their usual area. Your customers never show up as "new."

03 — Get in earlier

Reach builders before they pick a supplier.

Where a city publishes it, the feed includes permits still in review, not only issued ones, and every builder is scored from 0 to 50 on job stage, how much lumber the work uses, how many jobs they have going, and whether their contact details are on file.

How it works

From public record to warm lead in three steps.

1

Pull

We query county and city permit portals on ArcGIS, Socrata, CKAN, and OpenDataSoft, and normalize every record to one format: contractor, job address, permit type, and status.

2

Score

Each permit is scored from 0 to 50 on its stage, lumber tier, builder activity and contact details, then filtered to your saved profile: your markets, delivery radius, job types and stages. A project's sub-permits count once, trade-only permits are set aside, and national builders who buy on central contracts drop to the bottom.

3

Deliver

A short email Monday morning led by what changed, with the full scored list attached as a CSV, email and phone included. Unlimited users on your account. Timberseek never calls or texts builders; the list is yours to work.

What you get

Contact details straight off the permit.

Market reports

See where the lumber is going.

Every market and every builder in it is ranked by lumber-weighted permit activity, so a county full of new homes outranks one full of kitchen remodels. Job values come with each market where the city publishes them.

Your search

Built around what you sell.

When you start, we capture your job profile: the job types you supply, the permit stages you want to hear about, job size, and the markets you cover. Your lead list is tuned to it.

Clean leads

Worth the phone call.

No electricians, pool contractors or permit-filing services. A project with ten sub-permits counts once, remodels are not counted as new homes, and mistyped job values are dropped.
